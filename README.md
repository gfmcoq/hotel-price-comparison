## Why the closest room isn't the cheapest room

Searching "near me" answers a geography question. Your phone has location, the booking app knows roughly where you are, and the results you get are properties within a radius, sorted by a mix of distance, availability and whatever the platform wants to sell through tonight. Cheap isn't the ranking factor.

Price the shortlist properly and the gaps show up immediately. OTA rate parity rules keep most big platforms within a few percent of each other on the same property — ShopBack's own Singapore travel team puts that spread at 3–8% for most properties. A US-side comparison from the same source is blunter: prices on the same room can differ 5–15% across Booking.com, Agoda, Hotels.com and Marriott, so the "cheapest" platform genuinely changes from booking to booking.

Two quirks worth knowing before you start comparing:

- **Apps and websites don't always show the same price.** Reviewers who've run the same room through both have found prices generally better inside the booking apps than on the desktop sites. Your "near me" search on a laptop may not be showing you the number the app would.
- **Logged-in prices are a thing.** Agoda in particular offers user-only discounts and secret deals that don't appear until you're signed in, which makes it more competitive on Asia-Pacific inventory.

## The trap: prices can move when you click through

This is the part that decides whether cashback helps you or hurts you. Several reviewers have documented the same thing: after activating cashback and clicking through, the price on the booking site went up.

One documented example: a New York hotel showing at $435/night on Agoda. After activating an upsized 6.5% cashback, the same room type and cancellation terms came up at $510 — a $75 increase against $33 in cashback, a net loss. Booking.com showed the identical price before and after activation in the same test, which made it the better route that day. In another case, a Trip.com deal at $460 came up at $500 after clicking through with 4.5% cashback attached, netting out more expensive than simply booking direct.

That doesn't mean cashback never works. It means the only number that decides anything is the price *after* cashback, not the percentage.

The method is boring and takes about three minutes:

1. Find your room on the booking site you'd normally use.
2. Write down (or screenshot) the total, including taxes and fees.
3. Activate cashback and click through.
4. Check the total again. If it's unchanged or cheaper, book. If it jumped by more than the cashback is worth, go back and book the cheap route.
5. If you're unsure, check the price on a second device — it can take a while for a jumped price to revert.

## When booking direct still wins

Cashback isn't automatically the right answer. There are cases where it doesn't apply or doesn't pay enough to matter:

- **Elite hotel status.** Breakfast, upgrades and late checkout can be worth $50–$150 a night at a luxury property, and those perks don't transfer through OTA bookings. If you're Marriott Gold or Hilton Diamond, the loyalty maths probably beats 6% back.
- **Same-day bookings.** OTA inventory gets thin and unreliable at short notice; hotel apps often release same-day deals the OTAs never see.
- **Groups of 5+ rooms.** Bookings are treated room by room, and the hotel will usually negotiate a group rate directly.
- **Stays of 7+ nights.** Long-stay apartments and direct landlord bookings often beat OTA pricing outright.
- **Boutique properties that aren't listed.** Some ryokan, small guesthouses and eco-lodges only sell direct or through local platforms.
- **Award redemptions.** Point redemptions don't earn cashback.
